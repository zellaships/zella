# Case Study Criteria Framework
## The Gospel

---

## I. STRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE

### 1. The Quantified Promise (Hero Section)
Lead with 2-4 quantified outcomes that ladder to business value. Numbers create credibility before narrative.

**Pattern:**
- Primary metric (revenue, GMS, growth %)
- Secondary metric (velocity, efficiency gain)
- Tertiary metric (user/customer impact)

**Examples:**
- "120% Experiment Velocity Growth"
- "$16M incremental revenue"
- "$6M Incremental GMS"

---

### 2. The Crisis Frame (Inciting Incident)
Frame the problem as a crisis worth solving. Urgency creates stakes.

**Pattern:** "By [YEAR], [ORGANIZATION] faced [FUNDAMENTAL CHALLENGE]..."

**Examples:**
- "Etsy's Product Organization faced a fundamental challenge"
- "Leadership vacuum after the VP departed"
- "Checkout remained largely unchanged...creating a hidden bottleneck"

---

### 3. Multi-Layered Problem Architecture
Diagnose problems at three levels. Shows strategic thinking.

| Dimension | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| Immediate | What's broken right now |
| Organizational | Cultural/structural dysfunction |
| Systemic | Root cause / pattern |

---

### 4. Phased Solution Revelation
Structure solutions as sequential discoveries. Mirrors how work actually happens.

| Phase | Function |
|-------|----------|
| 1. Diagnosis | Reframe the problem |
| 2. Framework | Apply methodology |
| 3. Execution | Test and iterate |
| 4. Institutionalization | Embed in culture |

---

### 5. The Seven-Act Narrative Arc

| Act | Function |
|-----|----------|
| 1. Inciting Incident | Establish crisis/stakes |
| 2. Rising Action | Layer problems with research |
| 3. Turning Point | Introduction of new method |
| 4. Development | Framework application |
| 5. Climax | Key decision/breakthrough |
| 6. Resolution | Results quantified |
| 7. Reflection | Learnings crystallized |

---

## II. LANGUAGE MECHANISMS

### Causal Bridges
Connect problem → insight → solution:
- "This inconsistency stemmed from..."
- "To address this, [ORG] needed..."
- "Recognizing the need for a fundamental shift..."
- "Armed with this insight..."

### Temporal Anchors
Ground the reader in time:
- "By [YEAR]..."
- "Between [YEAR] and [YEAR]..."
- "Within [TIMEFRAME]..."
- "Years later, when I led..."

### Tension Acknowledgment
Show you understand complexity:
- "However, despite its critical role..."
- "Unlike previous experiments..."
- "While [ALTERNATIVE] might work for others..."

### Forward Motion
Keep momentum toward resolution:
- "With a clear understanding of the problem..."
- "Once deployed, our experiments yielded..."
- "Looking ahead...the strategy will continue to serve..."

### Quantified Proof Points
Embed numbers throughout (not just hero):
- "[BEFORE] → [AFTER]" (transformation)
- "[X]% overdelivery against target"
- Analogies for scale: "as long as a *New York Times* article"

---

## III. ROLE POSITIONING TECHNIQUES

### 1. Credibility Stacking
Layer multiple sources of authority:
- **Institutional**: "Founding member of..."
- **Relational**: "Recruited by [EXECUTIVE] to..."
- **Intellectual**: "I've come to think of as..."
- **Experiential**: "When I was a member of..."

### 2. Collaborative Credit
Avoid solo heroics:
- "close partnership with..."
- "co-create"
- Credit squads while maintaining visible strategic ownership
- D.I.W.O. (Do It With Others) ethos

### 3. Voice Oscillation
Alternate between observer and architect:
- Observer: "I first learned..."
- Architect: "we introduced..."
- Advocate: "advocated for..."

### 4. Servant-Leadership Framing
Position through testimonials:
- "source of inspiration"
- "clarity"
- "created psychologically safe spaces"

## IV. FRAMEWORK CITATION PATTERN

Cite 5-8 established methodologies with proper attribution. Shows intellectual range.

| Framework | What It Signals |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Build-Measure-Learn | Startup credibility |
| Design Sprint | Industry standard |
| Empowered Teams (Cagan) | Product leadership |
| Service Blueprint | Systems thinking |
| Gestalt/Bauhaus | Design fundamentals |
| Kotter's Change Model | Organizational savvy |
| Jobs to Be Done | Customer-centric |

---

## V. VISUAL DESCRIPTION TECHNIQUE

Describe what visuals *mean* before what they *show*. Use analogies for scale.

**Pattern:** "(Left: [CONCEPT]... Right: [CONTRAST]...)"

**Examples:**
- "Left: A sprawling Gantt chart... Right: A Basecamp thread..."
- "stretched nearly 3,000 pixels tall—about as long as a *New York Times* article"

---

## VI. IMPACT QUANTIFICATION LADDER

Results must scale from direct to strategic:

| Level | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Direct metric | 0.07% conversion lift |
| Contextualized | "6.56% of annual $90M target" |
| Business outcome | $6M GMS |
| Strategic value | "multi-year roadmap" |

---

## VII. OPENING TEMPLATES

```
"By [YEAR], [ORGANIZATION]'s [DOMAIN] faced a fundamental challenge: [CRISIS]."

"[DOMAIN] remained largely unchanged while [BROADER CONTEXT] evolved, creating [TENSION]."

"After [CRISIS EVENT], [ORGANIZATION] faced [SITUATION] that threatened [STAKES]."
```

---

## VIII. CLOSING TEMPLATES

```
"Looking ahead, [STRATEGY/FRAMEWORK] will continue to serve as a north star for [FUTURE WORK]."

"This experience crystallized key learnings about [THEME]: [INSIGHT]."

"Years after [TRANSFORMATION], the principles remain embedded in how [ORGANIZATION] [OPERATES]."
```
